Why The Porch Project

I grew up in a small town in Georgia built around a square with the little shops, the services, the place you ran your errands and somehow always stayed twenty minutes longer because someone stopped to talk. Nobody called it community building. It was just Tuesday.

After seven years here, here's what I've found: there is connection in Steiner Ranch. The hard part isn't always finding it, it's keeping up with it. We're all busy. We all mean to reach out. And somehow the garage door closes and another week goes by.

So we've all leaned on the same place to make up for it in the neighborhood Facebook groups. And they're a major point of connection; I hope they never stop. But they're built for conversation, and a conversation is a river. Sure, you can search it if you remember the exact words someone used and which of the four groups they posted in. Someone shares the handyman you'll definitely need, you screenshot it, and a week later you're scrolling four hundred photos of your kids trying to find it. (I do the same thing with events — screenshot the flyer, then completely forget it's happening.)

A town square holds things. So that's where The Porch Project starts: a trusted, neighbors-only directory where nothing gets buried, plus an events calendar where every event saves straight to your phone — so it's not on you to remember.

It's early, and growing every day. Come see what's there — and tell a neighbor.
